Financial Literacy

Learn budgeting, saving, and responsible borrowing

Why Financial Literacy Matters

Building money management skills for lifelong success

Our Commitment

WAIS is committed to helping students develop essential money management skills. Our financial literacy program provides educational resources, workshops, and activities to empower students with knowledge for lifelong financial success.

Understanding how to manage money is a critical skill that extends beyond your academic years. We provide student-friendly materials to help you build a strong financial foundation.

Program Benefits

  • Practical money management skills
  • Budgeting techniques for students
  • Saving strategies for academic goals
  • Responsible borrowing practices
  • Financial planning basics
  • Workshops and learning activities

Learning Topics

Key areas of financial education

Budgeting

Learn how to create and stick to a budget that works with your student allowance. Understand income, expenses, and how to prioritize spending.

Key Concepts:

  • Tracking income and expenses
  • Creating a student budget
  • Prioritizing needs vs. wants
  • Adjusting budget as needed

Saving

Discover the importance of saving and develop strategies to build your savings over time. Learn how to set and achieve savings goals.

Key Concepts:

  • Setting savings goals
  • Building emergency funds
  • Regular saving habits
  • Tracking progress

Responsible Borrowing

Understand when and how to borrow money responsibly. Learn about loan terms, interest, and repayment strategies.

Key Concepts:

  • Understanding loan terms
  • Assessing borrowing needs
  • Planning for repayment
  • Avoiding excessive debt

Financial Management

Develop overall financial management skills to make informed decisions about money throughout your life.

Key Concepts:

  • Financial goal setting
  • Making informed financial decisions
  • Understanding financial products
  • Planning for the future

Quick Tips for Students

Simple steps to improve your financial habits

1

Track Your Spending

Keep a record of where your money goes each month. This helps you identify areas where you can cut back.

2

Save Regularly

Set aside a small amount from your allowance regularly. Even small savings add up over time.

3

Plan Before You Spend

Before making purchases, ask yourself if it's a need or a want. Plan your spending accordingly.

4

Understand Before You Borrow

If you need to borrow, make sure you understand the terms, interest, and repayment schedule.

5

Build an Emergency Fund

Set aside money for unexpected expenses. This prevents you from relying on loans for emergencies.

6

Keep Learning

Financial literacy is a journey. Continue learning about money management as your situation changes.
